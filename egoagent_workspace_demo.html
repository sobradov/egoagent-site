Stage 5 of 6 · Your process note

Read, think, and save one note for the day.

Work on it as on paper. On a larger screen the transcript and the analyses sit side by side; tapping a passage of an analysis lights up the lines of the transcript it reads. Tap any passage, or highlight a few words, in either one. Add your own comment, or ask a question and keep the answer. Write your own thoughts at the end. Use any of these, or none, on any day.

1 · The transcript

Fri 14 Aug · 48 minutes · verbatim
P: I keep coming back to the drawing I ruined on Friday. I knew it was finished and I kept adding to it anyway.
T: You knew it was finished.
P: It's like I can't let anything be done. If it's done, someone can look at it and decide what it is.
T: And here?
P: Here too, I suppose. I always want one more session before you know what I am.

2 · The analyses

Another perspective, like a colleague's consultation

General psychoanalytic

Ruining a drawing she knew was finished suggests a conflict around completion: what is finished can be seen, and judged.
“Someone can decide what it is” places the judgement outside her, in another's eyes, where she cannot control it.

Ego psychology

Adding to a finished drawing may work as undoing: a defence that keeps the anxiety of being judged at bay.
“One more session before you know what I am” carries the same defence into the treatment: postponing the moment of being known.
